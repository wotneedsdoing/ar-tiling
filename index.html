Tile guide

Every type of tile, and where it works

Whatever you pick at the tile shop, we fit it. Here's every common type of tile, where it suits, and what it typically costs to buy. Prices are for the tiles only, per square metre.

Ceramic

Glazed clay tiles. Light, easy to cut and the widest choice of colours. Best on walls and light-use floors.

Best for
Bathroom and kitchen walls

Denser and harder than ceramic, and soaks up almost no water. Strong enough for any floor, wet room or heated floor.

£20–£90 /m²

Tiles from 600 × 600 mm up to 1200 × 600 mm and bigger slabs. Fewer grout lines and a clean, modern look. Needs a very flat surface and a levelling system.

Choosing your tiles

You pick them at the tile shop. We make sure they're right.

Most customers choose their own tiles at a tile shop. Here's everything to check before you buy, so the tiles suit the room, the right amount arrives, and the job goes perfectly.

Rectified
Edges ground perfectly square, so tiles can go with a tight 2 mm joint. Non-rectified tiles need wider grout lines.
PEI 1–5
How hard-wearing the glaze is. PEI 1–2 for walls only, PEI 3 for home floors, PEI 4–5 for hallways, kitchens and commercial floors.
R9–R13
Slip rating. R9 is fine for dry floors, R10–R11 for bathrooms, kitchens and outdoors, R12–R13 for commercial kitchens and ramps.
Absorption
How much water the tile soaks up. 0.5% or less is porcelain: best for showers, outdoors and frost.
Shade + batch
The most important thing on the box. Every box must have the same shade and batch number, or the colour can change halfway across the room.
m² per box
How many boxes to buy. Divide the "including extra" figure from our calculator by this number and round up.

Your tile shop checklist

  1. Measure first. Use our calculator for the area and how many tiles, and take the figures with you.
  2. Wall or floor? Floor tiles can go on walls, but wall tiles must never go on floors.
  3. Check the ratings on the box label: PEI for wear, R for slip, absorption for wet areas.
  4. Buy 10% extra, or 15% for herringbone, chevron or diagonal patterns.
  5. Same shade and batch on every box. Ask the shop to check before they load the van.
  6. Pick trims and grout to match, or let us advise on them.
  7. Delivery before day one. Have everything on site before we start.
  8. Keep a spare box for future repairs.

How it works

How a job runs

  1. Get in touch

    Send a few photos and the rough size of the room, or ask for a visit.

  2. Measure and quote

    Everything measured, then a clear written quote with the price and how long it takes.

  3. Prepare

    Old tiles off, walls boarded, floors levelled and wet areas tanked. This is what makes tiles last.

  4. Set out and tile

    Lines set out from the centre so cuts are even at both ends, then every tile laid true.

  5. Grout and seal

    Grouted, silicone sealed where it meets baths, trays and worktops, then cleaned down.

  6. Walk round

    A final look together before the job is signed off, with the mess cleared away.

Done properly

What goes under the tiles matters most

Most failed tiling isn't the tiles. It's what's underneath: plasterboard in a shower, too little adhesive, no room for movement. Every layer is done right here.

Tile + grout

Set out level, with even joints and cuts at both ends.

Adhesive

Full coverage on floors and large tiles, spread with the right notched trowel, so no hollow spots.

Tanking

A waterproof membrane in showers and wet rooms, sealed at every corner and pipe.

Backer board

Cement or foam board in wet areas, never plain plasterboard.

Movement joints

Gaps left at the edges and over large floors so tiles don't crack as the building moves.

Finishing touches

Grout, trims and aftercare

The details people notice: the colour of the grout, the edges, and how the tiles look in five years.

Grout

Picked to match or contrast with your tiles.

Bright white Silver grey Mid grey Anthracite Black Beige
  • Standard cement grout for most walls and floors
  • Epoxy grout for showers and worktops: waterproof and stain-proof
  • Flexible grout over underfloor heating and timber floors

Edges and trims

Every exposed edge finished cleanly, with a trim or a mitred corner.

  • Chrome, brushed steel, matt black or brushed brass
  • White or coloured trims to blend in
  • Mitred edges: tiles cut at 45° for a trim-free corner
  • Silicone sealed where tiles meet baths, trays and worktops

Aftercare

Simple care keeps tiles looking new.

  • Leave new grout and silicone at least 24 hours before using a shower
  • Clean with a pH-neutral cleaner; avoid bleach and acid on stone and grout
  • Natural stone resealed every one to two years
  • Re-silicone when it starts to mark or lift

FAQs

Questions people ask

How much does tiling cost?

It depends on the size of the area, the tiles you choose and how much preparation is needed underneath. Send a few photos and rough measurements for a free, no-obligation quote.

Do you supply the tiles?

Most customers choose their own tiles at a tile shop. Send us the tile's name or a photo of the box first and we'll check it suits the job and tell you how many boxes to buy, free. [Confirm: can AR Tiling also supply tiles?]

Is any tile or job too difficult?

No. Large-format slabs, natural stone, mosaics, glass, herringbone, wet rooms, outdoor porcelain: whatever you choose, we fit it.

Do you work outside Manchester?

Yes. We do homes and small businesses across Greater Manchester, and commercial contracts anywhere in the UK with our own team.

How does the 10% loyalty discount work?

After your first job, every job you book with us is 10% off our price for labour and the materials we supply. Recommend a friend and they get 10% off their first job too. It doesn't apply to tiles you buy yourself or to commercial contracts, which are priced per project.

Can you take the old tiles off?

Yes. Old tiles are removed and the walls or floor prepared, so the new tiles go onto a sound, flat surface.

How long will my job take?

It depends on the size and the preparation. Your quote says how many days the job will take before any work starts.

Can you tile over underfloor heating?

Yes, using flexible adhesive and grout made for heated floors, with the heating off while it sets.

Do you do small jobs?

Yes: splashbacks, re-grouting, replacing cracked tiles and other small repairs as well as full rooms. Small jobs have a minimum charge of around £150.

Ceramic or porcelain: which should I choose?

Ceramic is great for walls and costs less. Porcelain is harder and soaks up almost no water, so it's the better choice for floors, showers, wet rooms and anywhere with underfloor heating.

Can you tile over my old tiles?

Sometimes, if the old tiles are flat and stuck down firmly. In showers, wet rooms and on most floors it's better to take them off, so the new tiles bond properly and last.

How soon can I use my shower?

Leave new grout and silicone at least 24 hours before using the shower. If a job needs longer, your quote will say so.

Do my tiles need sealing?

Porcelain and glazed ceramic don't. Natural stone such as marble, travertine and limestone does, when it's laid and every one to two years after that.

Does herringbone cost more?

Yes, a little. Herringbone, chevron and diagonal patterns take more cuts and setting out, so laying costs around 20 to 30% more, and you'll need about 15% extra tiles instead of 10%.
