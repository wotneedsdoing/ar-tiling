How much does it cost to tile a bathroom?

Typical 2026 prices outside London, what changes them, and how to keep the cost down without cutting corners.

5 minute read · by the AR Tiling team · October 2026

The short answer

Most bathrooms cost between about £35 and £65 per m² to tile, before the tiles themselves. That covers our labour (about £25 to £45 per m²) and the adhesive, grout and trims (about £10 to £20 per m²). Tiles add anything from £15 per m² for plain ceramic to £100 or more for natural stone and mosaics.

Add the tiles you choose, plus about 10% extra for cuts (15% for herringbone or diagonal patterns). The tile shop works all of this out for any tile.

What changes the price

  • Preparation. Removing old tiles, boarding walls, levelling floors and waterproofing showers can take as long as the tiling. We price it after seeing the room.
  • Tile size and type. Standard 300 to 600 mm tiles are quickest. Mosaics, large slabs, natural stone and handmade tiles take longer.
  • Pattern. Herringbone and diagonal layouts mean more cuts: allow 20% to 30% more labour.
  • Details. Niches, mitred corners, boxing-in and lots of pipes add time.

How to save without cutting corners

  • Choose standard-size tiles in a straight or brick layout.
  • Send us the tile before you buy, so nothing is wasted on the wrong tile.
  • Do the strip-out yourself if you're handy, and we'll check it before we start.
  • Book walls and floors together rather than as two visits.
  • Come back to us: returning customers and anyone they refer get 10% off fitting.

Never skip the waterproofing in a shower or the levelling under big tiles. Those are the jobs that stop leaks and cracked tiles, and the costliest to put right later.
