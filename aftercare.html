Caring for your new tiles

Look after them like this and they'll look as good in ten years as they do today. Your tiler writes the exact times on your handover sheet.

The first few days

When it's safe
WhatUsually after
Walking on a new floor24 hours (sooner with rapid-set products)
Using the shower or bath24 to 72 hours after grouting and silicone
Putting heavy furniture or appliances back48 to 72 hours, lifted, never slid
Switching on underfloor heatingThe date on your handover sheet, then a degree or two a day

Everyday cleaning

  • Warm water and a pH-neutral tile cleaner, then rinse and dry for a streak-free finish.
  • A squeegee on shower walls after every shower is the best way to stop limescale and mould.
  • Open a window or run the extractor fan for 20 minutes after bathing.

Never use

  • Acid or descaling cleaners (vinegar, lemon, limescale removers) on marble, limestone, travertine or other natural stone. They etch the surface.
  • Bleach or harsh chemicals on coloured grout. They fade it.
  • Wire wool, scourers or steam cleaners on gloss tiles, glass tiles or sealed stone.

Grout and silicone

  • Brush grout every few weeks with a soft brush and a mild grout cleaner.
  • Silicone is a maintenance item and may need replacing every few years. We can do it for you.
  • Natural stone and some grouts need resealing every 1 to 2 years. If water darkens the surface instead of beading, it's time.

Outdoor tiles

  • Brush off leaves and moss regularly and use a patio cleaner made for porcelain or stone.
  • Check the product before using rock salt on porcelain in winter.

Keep your spare tiles

We leave you a few spare tiles from the same batch. Keep them somewhere dry, so a broken tile can be swapped for a perfect match.
